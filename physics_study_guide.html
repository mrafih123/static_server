Capacitors & Cell Combinations - Comprehensive Notes

1. Introduction to Capacitors

A capacitor is a passive electronic component that stores electrical energy in an electric field. It consists of two conductive plates separated by an insulating material called a dielectric.

💡 Key Concept

When a capacitor is connected to a battery, it accumulates charge on its plates until the voltage across the capacitor equals the battery voltage. At this point, the current stops flowing.

Basic Properties

  • Capacitance (C): The ability of a capacitor to store charge, measured in Farads (F)
  • Voltage (V): The potential difference across the capacitor plates
  • Charge (Q): The amount of electrical charge stored, measured in Coulombs (C)

Fundamental Relationship:

$$Q = CV$$

Where:

  • \(Q\) = Charge stored (Coulombs)
  • \(C\) = Capacitance (Farads)
  • \(V\) = Voltage across capacitor (Volts)

2. Charging and Discharging of Capacitors

Charging Process

When a capacitor is initially uncharged and connected to a voltage source through a resistor:

  • Current flows maximum at the beginning
  • Charge builds up gradually on the plates
  • Voltage across capacitor increases exponentially
  • Current decreases as the capacitor charges

Charging Equations:

$$q(t) = Q_{\text{max}}\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right)$$ $$i(t) = \frac{V}{R}e^{-t/RC}$$ $$V_C(t) = V\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right)$$

Where \(RC\) is the time constant (τ), representing the time to reach 63.2% of full charge.

Discharging Process

When a charged capacitor is disconnected from the source and allowed to discharge through a resistor:

  • Current flows in the opposite direction
  • Charge decreases exponentially
  • Voltage across capacitor decreases
  • After full discharge, current becomes zero

Discharging Equations:

$$q(t) = Q_0 e^{-t/RC}$$ $$i(t) = -\frac{V_0}{R}e^{-t/RC}$$ $$V_C(t) = V_0 e^{-t/RC}$$
⚠️ Important Note: When a capacitor is fully charged, it behaves like an open circuit (no current flows). When fully discharged, it acts like a short circuit initially.

3. Energy Stored in a Capacitor

A charged capacitor stores electrical potential energy in its electric field. This energy can be released when needed.

Energy Formulas:

$$U = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C} = \frac{1}{2}QV$$

Where:

  • \(U\) = Energy stored (Joules)
  • \(C\) = Capacitance (Farads)
  • \(V\) = Voltage (Volts)
  • \(Q\) = Charge (Coulombs)

Change in Energy

When the capacitance or voltage changes, the change in stored energy is:

$$\Delta U = \frac{1}{2}C(V_f^2 - V_i^2)$$

4. Charged Capacitor as a Voltage Source

Once a capacitor is fully charged and disconnected from the charging source, it can act as a temporary voltage source itself.

💡 Important Behavior

When a charged capacitor is connected to an external resistor:

  • The capacitor acts as a voltage source with voltage \(V_0\)
  • Current flows through the resistor in the discharge direction
  • The charge on the capacitor decreases over time
  • Eventually, all stored energy is dissipated as heat in the resistor

Initial Conditions:

At the moment of connection (t = 0):

$$V_{\text{capacitor}} = V_0$$ $$I_{\text{initial}} = \frac{V_0}{R}$$ $$Q_{\text{initial}} = CV_0$$
⚠️ Practical Application: This principle is used in camera flashes, backup power supplies, and pulse-forming networks where quick energy release is needed.

5. Combination of Cells (Batteries)

Cells (batteries) can be connected in different configurations to achieve desired voltage and current characteristics.

Series Combination

When cells are connected end-to-end (positive to negative):

Cells in Series

Series Combination Formulas:

$$\mathcal{E}_{\text{eq}} = \mathcal{E}_1 + \mathcal{E}_2 + \mathcal{E}_3 + \cdots + \mathcal{E}_n$$ $$r_{\text{eq}} = r_1 + r_2 + r_3 + \cdots + r_n$$

Where:

  • \(\mathcal{E}_{\text{eq}}\) = Equivalent EMF (total voltage)
  • \(r_{\text{eq}}\) = Equivalent internal resistance
  • \(\mathcal{E}_i\) = EMF of individual cell
  • \(r_i\) = Internal resistance of individual cell

Key Points for Series Combination

  • Voltage adds up: Total EMF is the sum of individual EMFs
  • Internal resistance adds: Total internal resistance increases
  • Same current: Current through each cell is identical
  • Use case: Used when higher voltage is needed

6. Quick Reference Summary

Concept Formula Key Point
Capacitance \(C = \frac{Q}{V}\) Ability to store charge
Energy Stored \(U = \frac{1}{2}CV^2\) Energy in electric field
Time Constant \(\tau = RC\) 63.2% charging time
Cells in Series \(\mathcal{E}_{\text{eq}} = \sum \mathcal{E}_i\) Voltages add up
Charging \(q = Q_{\text{max}}(1-e^{-t/RC})\) Exponential growth
Discharging \(q = Q_0 e^{-t/RC}\) Exponential decay

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Remember that a fully charged capacitor blocks DC current (open circuit)
  • In series cell combinations, if one cell is reversed, subtract its EMF
  • The time constant τ = RC determines how fast charging/discharging occurs
  • Energy is always positive and proportional to the square of voltage
